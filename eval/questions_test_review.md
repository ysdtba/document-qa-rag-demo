## Summary

Twelve questions can stand as drafted: h16, h17, h19, h20, h21, h24, h25, a17, a18, a20, a24, a25. Four should be edited: h22, a16, a19, a21. Four are judgment calls about which page states the fact, or about a minus sign: h18, h23, a22, a23.

**a21.** I agree with changing the question to “in US cents” and the answer to **59.10**.

The report states basic EPS twice as **59.10 US cents** (p.40 and note 12 on p.213) and once as **US$0.59** (p.154). The dollar figure is the cents figure rounded to two decimal places. The scorer multiplies million/billion words only. It does not treat “cents” as a hundredth of a currency unit, and it does not convert. Against gold 0.59, the answer “59.10 US cents” is parsed as 59.10 and fails. Against gold 59.10, “US$0.59” and “US$0.591” fail. The cents form is the unrounded figure, it is the figure in the EPS note, and it is the same treatment already used for a07. On p.154, basic and diluted EPS are both printed as 0.59, so the dollar question cannot separate them. In cents they are 59.10 and 59.01.

I do not agree with gold_pages **[40, 154, 213]**. Pages 40 and 213 print 59.10. Page 154 prints 0.59, not 59.10. That is the same situation as the rounded “US$345 billion” line that was removed from a06: the page does not state the gold figure, and 0.59 is not within 0.5% of 59.10. Use **[40, 213]**.

Two scoring limits remain after that change. Diluted EPS of 59.01 US cents is within 0.5% of 59.10 (the tolerance band is about 0.30), so the number scorer cannot tell basic from diluted; the word “basic” in the question is what separates them. And because the scorer ignores “cents”, an answer of “US$59.10” matches the number 59.10 whether or not currency is set to USD. Leave currency blank, as on a07, and keep the unit in the question text.

**Other scoring notes.** h18 is the bracketed outflow: a leading minus fails, parentheses do not. a19’s 9,498 cash-flow total fails against 9,609. a16’s net CSM of 54,685 fails against 64,945. No other number question has a second presentation, in cents or in another currency, that a correct answer would be likely to use.

**Dev set.** No test question asks for a figure or a text fact already in `eval/questions.jsonl`. The nearest pairs are different facts: h18 versus h09, h19 versus h02, a18 versus a17, a21 versus h03, a23 versus the headcount on a09, and a24 versus a01 and a15.
